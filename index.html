The test case

A customer with a precise goal

Each record sets a persona, a goal, must-have constraints, a mock airline database, and the exact final database state that counts as success.

The conversation

Audio in both directions

A user simulator speaks with the agent over a live audio connection. The agent can read and change the isolated scenario database through tools.

The judgment

Outcome + experience

Validators first reject corrupted simulations. Then code and judges assess task accuracy, faithful behavior, spoken clarity, timing, brevity, and progress.

Quick vocabulary

Rebooking

Moving a passenger from one booked journey to another. The agent may need to quote fees and confirm the change first.

IRROPS

“Irregular operations”: a disruption such as a cancellation or major delay. It can trigger fee waivers, refunds, or vouchers depending on policy.

Ground truth

The exact expected final airline database state. EVA compares it with the state left by the agent’s actions.

Collection

What the runner saves

Separate caller, agent, and mixed WAV recordings; transcript JSONL; audit logs; Pipecat and simulator events; tool calls and responses; initial and final database snapshots. The metrics processor combines them into a chronological conversation_trace and turn-level fields.

Important distinction

Intended, spoken, heard

“Intended” text is what a speaker meant to say. Audio is what was spoken. “Transcribed” text is what the listener’s recognition system heard. Keeping all three exposes STT and speech generation errors that a text-only test would miss.

How to read these together

A smooth call can still produce the wrong booking. EVA therefore reports task accuracy and conversational experience separately, with diagnostic measures to help locate a failure.

Why a single trial is not enough

For one scenario, toggle which of five independent trials passed a dimension. The public results average each statistic across scenarios.

Pass rate vs mean

Pass@1 is the fraction of trials where every metric in a dimension meets its cutoff. Mean averages normalized component scores. One missed threshold can make pass@1 zero for a trial even if its mean looks strong.

The site reports pass@5 as the share of scenarios with at least one successful trial, and pass⁵ as the average estimated probability of five consecutive successes, (c/5)⁵.

What the examples represent

Scenario cards and reference traces come from published dataset records. The observed 1.1.2 call is a repository test fixture, shown as an example of recorded artifacts. The score lab is hypothetical and does not claim a model result.

Limits to keep in mind

The airline slice has 50 controlled mock cases. Judges can make mistakes; commercial model access, voice synthesis, network timing, and validation retries affect reproduction. The official leaderboard can change after this guide’s source snapshot.
